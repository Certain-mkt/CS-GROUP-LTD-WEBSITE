01 — Vetting and training

Who is standing at your gate?

Guards are vetted to Private Security Regulatory Authority criteria, must be literate, and pass a physical and medical examination. Vetting happens before training, not after deployment.

  • PSRA criteria — the regulatory vetting standard for private security officers in Kenya.
  • Literacy and medical checks — reading, writing and physical fitness for the post.
  • One month of training — access control, firefighting drills, first aid, crime detection and prevention, and customer care.
  • Practical induction — time beside experienced guards before taking a post alone.
  • Written record — you receive details of every guard assigned to your site.

Training is not a certificate on a wall.

Before a guard is posted alone, they work alongside an experienced officer on a live site. The induction is practical because the job is practical.

02 — Supervision

What happens between handovers.

Most security failures happen when nobody is checking. Supervision is built into the contract, not added when something goes wrong.

  • Three checks per shift — supervisors visit each guard three times, day and night.
  • Unannounced spot checks — duty managers arrive by company vehicle without warning.
  • Immediate replacement — if a post is uncovered, a replacement guard is posted straight away.
  • Radio or mobile contact — supervisors and site in-charges stay reachable through the shift.

A guard who is not supervised is a person sitting at a gate.Current Security operating principle

Ask for the supervision record for your site during the first month. If it is not being done, you should see it in the log before you see it in an incident report.

03 — Transition

Changing providers should not leave a gap.

We aim to take over smoothly from your current provider within 12 hours of the contract starting. A resident contract manager oversees the transition and stays with the account.

  1. Survey

    Site visit and written recommendations.

  2. Instructions

    Assignment instructions agreed with you.

  3. Inventory

    Property and keys recorded at the site.

  4. Introduction

    Guards introduced before posting.

  5. Handover

    Contract manager manages the changeover.

What you receive

Written recommendations, the assignment instructions for each post, a property inventory and the details of every guard assigned.

What we need from you

Access to the site, a contact for day-to-day decisions, and any existing post instructions or incident history. The more we know, the less the first week costs you.

04 — Cover and compliance

Paperwork that survives a claim.

Security is a risk transfer. The cover behind the contract matters as much as the guard on the gate.

  • Fire, theft and burglary — cover for the risks a guarding contract is meant to address.
  • WIBA — Work Injury Benefits Act cover for the officers on your site.
  • Contractual cover — cover tied to the obligations in the contract.
  • PROSAK membership — Protective Security Association of Kenya.
  • PSRA licensing — licence number PSRA/CSL/19/90 and certificates available on request.

Procurement checklist

Ask any security provider for their PSRA licence, insurance certificates, guard vetting process and supervision records. A credible provider will hand them over without a fight.

05 — Verification

Anyone can wear a uniform.

Every Current Security officer carries a PSRA card and a company staff ID. If the person at your gate cannot produce both, do not rely on the uniform.

  • PSRA card — photo, registration number and expiry date.
  • Company ID — name, staff number and assigned post.
  • Confirmation call — we check the posting against our deployment record.

06 — Questions

Frequently asked questions

If the answer you need is not here, call the hotline or send a message. We will give you a straight answer.

Ask a question
How quickly can you start guarding our premises?

We aim to take over smoothly from your current provider within 12 hours of the contract starting. A site survey is done at the earliest possible time, and a resident contract manager oversees the transition.

How are your guards selected and trained?

Guards are vetted to Private Security Regulatory Authority criteria, must be literate, and pass a physical and medical examination. They complete one month of training covering access control, firefighting drills, first aid, crime detection and prevention, and customer care, then work alongside experienced guards before taking up a post alone.

How do you make sure guards stay alert on shift?

Supervisors check each guard three times per shift, day and night. Duty managers also carry out unannounced spot checks by company vehicle and can post replacement guards straight away if there is a gap. Supervisors and site in-charges carry radios or mobile phones.

Are you insured?

Yes. We carry fire, theft, burglary, WIBA and contractual cover. Ask for the current certificates during procurement.

Can I hire guards for a short period or an event?

Yes. We offer temporary guard service by the day or night, as well as monthly contracts. Contact us with the dates and location and we will quote.

How do I get a quote?

Use the quote form on the contact page, message us on WhatsApp, or call the hotline. We survey your premises, give written security recommendations, and then send a quotation tailored to your site.

Where do you operate?

Our offices are in Nairobi and Sirisia, Bungoma, and we serve clients across Kenya. Call us to confirm coverage for your area.

Do you work with the police?

Yes. We are a member of the Protective Security Association of Kenya (PROSAK) and work closely with the police, local administration and other private security providers.

What if I have a problem with the service?

Raise it first with your resident contract manager or site supervisor. For anything urgent, call the hotline. Please also confirm the issue in writing by email so it is on record and we can act on it quickly.

Ask us the difficult questions.

Licence, insurance, vetting, supervision records. We will show you the paperwork.
